# The map

Deeper reference than `CLAUDE.md`. Read that first for the build loops and the
working agreement; read this when you need to find something or need the
vocabulary. It is a map, not a copy of the code -- the code is the truth, and
anything here that contradicts it is out of date.

## What this is

One repo, served straight from its root by GitHub Pages. No server, no framework, no
bundler. Four sites have pages: `nflbets/` (X NFL Bets and Stats, built by
`nflbets/build/build.js` from the prop model's parts, the betting app, the X Parlays
section, the parlay card, the storage layer and the Player Elo tab), `news/`, `cfb/` and `nhl/`. The pages carry no season
data: each fetches what the scheduled jobs commit (`props/data/payload.json`,
`betting/state.json`, `elo/data/*.json`, `cfb/state.json` and `cfb/news.json`,
`nhl/state.json`) and what Claude commits from what the owner sends (`liveparlays/parlays.json`,
`liveparlays/xbets.json`) on every load. `news/` alone keeps its data in plain
script files, so it works from `file://`. Six scheduled GitHub Actions jobs, one per model
or site, rebuild the data from their sources and commit it.

`index.html` at the root is a static landing page linking the four. `pickems/` and `live/`
hold only redirects for old bookmarks; `liveparlays/` holds a redirect too, beside the X
Parlays section's source and `parlays.json`.

```
nflbets/   X NFL Bets and Stats      the one NFL site: both models, the Pick'ems board, Player Elo
props/     Prop Model                no page: the parts, build, job and payload.json
betting/   X NFL Betting Model       no page: the app source, tools, job and state.json; framed into nflbets/
elo/       Player Elo                no page: build.py, job and data/
news/      Season Tracker            newsletter-style week-by-week site
cfb/       X College Football Bets   a test site: moneylines and spreads
nhl/       X NHL Bets                moneylines, puck lines and totals
```

Picks, bankroll, bets, odds and parlays a visitor makes stay in that browser's local
storage. X's parlays and the X Bet Log are files in the repository (`liveparlays/parlays.json`,
`liveparlays/xbets.json`), read only by every device (below). Nothing on any page writes anywhere
but the visitor's own browser; everything else changes only with a commit.

## Bets and Stats: one page over both models

`nflbets/` (X NFL Bets and Stats) is the one site: the props and betting sites have no pages
of their own any more, only their source, tools, jobs and data. `nflbets/build/build.js` assembles the prop model
from its parts (title, heading and tab bar swapped; every prop model section kept in the
page, listeners and all, whether or not it has a button yet), sets the Pick'ems board in
front of it from `nflbets/build/tab_pickems.html` (its own section, styles and closure,
every class and id prefixed `pk-`), and sets the betting app's Records, Power Ratings
and Bet Log (the X Bet Log) tabs into frames: the app as `betting/tools/build.js` builds it is carried in
the page as a string and becomes a frame's srcdoc when its tab is first opened, with
`window.EMBED_TAB` (the tab, which also hides the app's header and tab bar) and
`window.STATE_URL` (`../betting/state.json`) written in front of it. A srcdoc frame is the
page's own origin, so the app keeps its browser store, and nothing is fetched or cached
for it apart from the page. The frame follows its content's height (a ResizeObserver on its body)
and its document never scrolls up and down by itself (`overflow-y: hidden` on its root, set as it
loads), so no classic scrollbar can stick in it when the content measured with a bar comes out
taller without one; sideways it still scrolls where something is really wider. The board's tag
and confidence bands are lifted from the betting app at build time and scoped inside the
closure, since the prop model has its own `tag()`.
The prop model's tabs run on the prop model's own state under its own storage key, and
the framed tabs on the betting app's, so a pick, parlay or bet made in either is what
this page shows. Tabs are addresses: `#slate`, `#parlay` (the Parlay Builder), `#xparlays`
(X Parlays), `#record` and so on (`routeTabs()` in `tab_pickems.html`). On a phone the tab bar is
wider than the screen and scrolls sideways, so on load, on a new address and on a tap the chosen
button is brought into the bar by the bar's own `scrollLeft`, never by scrolling the page.
The header's `buildTag` is the prop model's `APP_BUILD` and the page's own hash (`PAGE_HASH`,
seven hex of the page's SHA-256 taken with a placeholder in its slot), so every source change
shows. `smoke.js` builds the page in memory and fails unless the published files match it, checks
the hash, and runs `betting/tools/reference_models.json`'s gate on the `BET_APP` string.
The Pick'ems board is Vegas's call on nflverse's lines from `state.json`; it takes its season
from `state.json`, reads each week's scoreboard in ESPN's numbering (weeks 19-22, named Wild Card
to Super Bowl, are seasontype 3), says the regular season is over once every game is graded and
the schedule has no playoff game, reads "vs" for a neutral site (nflverse's Neutral or a 9:30am
Eastern kickoff), and says, under an opened game, when the prop model's Game bets are priced on a
different spread from the board's.
An opened game has a **Game snapshot** button at its head, over the prices. It opens
`nflbets/build/snapshot.html`'s window (`#gsModal`, every name `gs-`, its script one closure that
the build sets in last, after the Player Elo tab's; the page's modal style, Escape, Close or a
click outside closes it, Tab stays inside, the focus goes back to the button, full screen and
scrolling on a phone) with everything the site has on that one game, at a glance first. Its header
is pinned and, once the verdict's line has scrolled under it, shows the game in one line instead
(`#gsMini`: each team's record and the spread, or the score; hidden from a screen reader, the dialog
keeps its title). The verdict (`#gsMatch`, always shown) has each team's record as Team Rankings
counts it, the score or the kickoff in the viewer's time and the site, a played game's note ruled in
gold under the score, the headline -- the Vegas favourite's nickname and the spread it is favoured
by, its chance and the board's own confidence pill (`BET.tier`), or once played the winner with an
Upset or Favourite won chip and the line it was favoured by under it -- over the board's own split
bar, the spread, total and moneylines (one row on every phone, the moneylines one over the other)
with one line naming the sources, and five answer lines (`.gs-glance`), each a button
(`data-gs-goto`) that opens its card and brings it under the header: the model picks with Vegas kept
apart as the baseline (how many of the five models agree with it, the ones that do not named with
their dots, the ones with no call yet, how many of the six calls were right once played), the Team
Elo gap with both ratings and ranks, which side ranks higher on offense against defense each way with
the four ranks, who is out by name and how many starters are questionable, and the top mismatch with
its edge on a four-step meter. Under it six cards, each a button (`[data-gs-fold]`, `aria-expanded`)
with its title, a badge where a count helps (Starters: the ranked players out; Mismatches: how many)
and a one-line summary over a body that folds (Every call and Team strength open on a wide screen,
every card folded on a phone, Open all and Close all; nothing saved, nothing redrawn): every call
(`#gsCalls`, one lean chart: a row a call, its bar under its name and pick, leaning from 50/50 toward
the side its call picks, as far as its chance, in that team's colour -- Vegas, Alpha Model, the
Challenger Model, the Joker, the ELO Model, the Broly Model -- graded once the game is played:
`processed` once the job has graded it, else `atKickoff`, `joker` and `broly` in `state.json` and
`graded` or `next` in `elo/data/model.json`); Team strength (each team's Power Rating, rank, shield,
change and chance against an average team, from `teams`, ranked as Team Rankings ranks them);
Offense vs defense, Overall Offense against Overall Defense both ways ("When the Eagles have the
ball": `units`, the overall rank and rating first, and folded under one button, `[data-gs-sub]`,
the stats either rating weighs paired offense against defense, each rank over its value);
the side of every comparison with the edge wears one mark, `gs-better`;
Starters (the team's ranked players out first, each group's `sidelined`, the box red when someone
is; then each team's expected starters, `lineups` in `matchups.json`, side by side under each
position on Offense, Defense and Kicker tabs -- `role="tablist"`, the arrow keys, Home and End, the
choice kept while the page is open -- each with his season Elo, rank and shield as on the ELO
Ratings tab and a Q from the report, the higher Elo of each pair marked, and each defensive group's
header carrying both teams' units as the matchup formula ranks them among the league's); the
Mismatches in the game (the Props tab's own rows, `window.eloMismatches`, ranked among the week's,
each edge on the four-step meter with its legend); and when each file was published, with
links that close the window and open Team Rankings, ELO Ratings, Props on this game and the Pick'em
Record. One type scale (11, 13, 15, 18, 22 and 30px, custom properties on `#gsModal`; a phone takes
the top two to 20 and 26) and every control at least 44px tall; red text on the red tint (the Upset
chip, the out badge and heading) is a darker red, `--gs-miss-ink` (5.3:1 there, against the page's
red at 4.4:1), and the Big mismatch grade's green a shade darker than the ELO tab's (4.9:1 on white,
against 4:1); the preview's dark theme lifts the lean bars' team colours toward white, the mismatch
grades' colours, the board's Medium pill, the window's muted text (to 5:1 on its panels) and its red
(`--miss` and `--gs-miss-ink` to #FF8AA3, 5.3:1 on the panels and 4.7:1 on the tint) so they read
there: every text in the window, every card open, is at least 4.5:1 on what it sits on in both looks. It computes nothing of its own beyond
the vote's counts and the Elo gap, each
marked `data-gs-n` and held by the smoke: the board hands it the season as loaded, its Vegas call and
where a game stands (`window.pkBoard`), and the Player Elo tab its one read of `elo/data/`
(`window.eloFiles`), so no file is fetched twice. A played game says the ratings and lineups are
the files as they stand now; a game outside the matchups' week, a game under way, a lineup for a
team's next game rather than this one, and a file that did not load are each said in their card,
and the rest still draws. `smoke.js`'s last section (THE GAME SNAPSHOT) holds every number to its
file, on a game to come, a final on the scoreboard, a graded game and a playoff game, with
matchups.json or the ratings missing, read with the cards, the stats fold and the Starters tabs as
the window opens (so a folded card or a tab not shown is held like the rest); the headline to the
files and its confidence pill to the board's own; the dissenters, the badges, the edge marks in every
card, among the starters and on the verdict's Team Elo and Offense vs defense answers (the higher
rating, the better rank of each offense and defense that meet, the side named with the edge), and the
unit ranks in their headers; the bars, to the same numbers (each call's bar leaning toward its pick,
`gs-toh` home and `gs-toa` away, as far as its chance and in that team's colour; the split bar under
the headline each side's Vegas chance wide); the game bar; the answers' buttons, the folding,
the tabs' keys and kept choice, the Tab order and the 44px tap heights. The position table is `table-layout: fixed` (each team half
the card) and its names wrap: laid out automatically, a long name set a column's width and pushed

### X's parlays and the X Bet Log

Both are files in the repository, beside each other in `liveparlays/`, and every device reads them,
read only, on every load (`cache: 'no-store'`). There is no owner, no sign-in and no shared store:
nothing on the page writes anywhere but the visitor's own browser storage.

- **`liveparlays/parlays.json`** is X Parlays: `games` (the game ids, listed once), `parlays` (each
  an `id`, `week`, `stake`, `price`, `payout`, an optional `note`, and `legs` pointing at a game by
  its place in `games`, with the player as ESPN spells him, the team, the stat, the line, the side
  and `main`) and `updated`. Claude keeps it from the slips the owner sends in chat: a new slip is a
  new parlay; a line the book moved before the bet is written as the line X took; a parlay the owner
  no longer wants on the page gets `"cleared": true`, which keeps it in the file as history and off
  the page. Its `how` field says all of this, for whoever edits it next.
- **`liveparlays/xbets.json`** is the X Bet Log: `season` (the betting app's; a file for another
  season shows no weeks and says which it holds), `deposit` (for the balance; `null` for none, and
  the chart is then X's profit against break even), `weeks` keyed `w1` to `w22`, each `staked`,
  `returned` (everything that came back, stakes included) and an optional plain-text `note`, and
  `updated`. Claude adds or changes a week from what the owner sends. `nflbets/build/xbets.js` reads
  it, cleans it (a week 1-22, amounts of 0 or more, a note without angle brackets, 140 characters

  the frames must not be sandboxed); `betting/tools/build.js`'s hook lays the weeks into the app's

  and deposit in the app's key exactly as they were, whatever a frame saves.

`nflbets/build/storage.js`, inlined before the prop model's script, defines the `window.storage`
the prop model saves through (this browser's localStorage and nothing else, its builder and saved
parlays read back without `<`, `>`, `"` and a backtick, as the page always read them), and takes

corrected (`file|w3-dk-sgp-5|4` at 239.5, which is the file's), deletions, betting slips it deleted
itself. Anything else in it -- any other corrected line (on a parlay of the browser's own, on another

Until 10 October 2026 both lived in a Firebase Realtime Database written from the owner's devices
(an owner link and Firebase sign-in); its last state was carried into the two files that day (the

nflverse's rows, and a row of zeros marked `from_snaps` for a player who took a snap in his role with no
stat line), `snaps` (who took part, `snaps[week][game][club]`: the players with no stat line who took a
snap of any kind, a club for every game whose snap counts and stats are in; `who_played` in `weekly.py`,
from nflverse's snap counts; `settleLeg` grades such a player on zero, one his club's list leaves out
void, and waits where there is no club, unless the report had him Out), `corr`
(the pair correlation table: 329 entries, 341 since the quarterback's touchdown joined in app v102), `model`/`grid`/`dist`/`qs`/`k`/`prior`/`pts` (fitted
model pieces), `mkt_scale` and `tdrate` (from `data/scale.json` and `data/tdrate.json`) and

spread), `injuries` (this week's report in full, with practice status, and earlier weeks'
Outs and Doubtfuls), `roster` (every skill player's team, roster status, name and the week
the row is for, then where they differ his football name with his surname (or null) and
his legal form, `names_of` in `names.py`, which the audit matches book
names against, and anyone else the page can show, whatever position the roster gives him),
`depth` + `depth_dt`, `price_pull` + `credits` (the last price pull), `dk` (DraftKings' own id for
every outcome the pulls read on a game still to come, `dk[week][game][key]`, the key a leg's own
without its game, then its side and DraftKings' point: `<pid>|<stat>|over|79.5`, `<pid>|any_td`,
`team:BAL|ml`, `team:ATL|ats|-3`, `game|total|under|43.5`, and `event`, DraftKings' event id, with `at`
and `glat`, when the game's player prices and its game lines were read; `dk_ids`
in `weekly.py` builds it from `data/dk_links_wk{W}.json`, a player placed by the rule his prices are;
the parlay card's Open in DraftKings and Share link read it), `season`, `week` and
`season_over`, `not_posted` (`stats` and/or `injuries` when nflverse has not posted this
season's file yet: the slate says so, and the audit expects none of it), `build` (the data
build) and `baked_at`. Only this season's games are baked: a price or line row of another
season's game is dropped, and a payload of another season carries no lines forward. A `sched` game
carries `ls` (`dk` or `nflverse`: whose moneyline, spread and total it has) and `lat` (when
DraftKings' were pulled); DraftKings' are used only while under a day old. Its total `tot` comes
with the over and under prices `tov`/`tou` (nflverse's `over_odds`/`under_odds`, or DraftKings'

status by player) and `INJ` (this week's tags: Q, did not practise, limited), with the ruled

inactive, and a reserve list, a release or a retirement, kept as `week:'season'`). The season replays week by week, each week graded
on its own injury report, before today's roster and report are applied.

what gets serialised to local storage -- anything that must not persist has to
live outside it.

Sections. part1.html's own tab bar (Games, Parlay Builder, Track Record, Weekly Update,

bar. `tab-slate` is the Props tab (`slateView` + the `gameModal` overlay), `tab-parlay` is

button, kept because the prop model draws into them: `tab-track` (Track Record, the Prop

### Vocabulary

- **leg** -- one line on a parlay. Keyed `gid|pid|stat` (`legKey()`), plus a
  threshold `k` and a `side` (`over`/`under`). One line per player per stat.
- **main line** -- the real sportsbook over/under for a stat (`main:true`).
- **rung** -- a step on a **threshold ladder**, the "10+, 20+, 30+" style
  alternatives (`LADDER` in `part2.js`). Hidden behind a checkbox by default.
- **anytime touchdown** -- `any_td`, a yes/no leg (`prob` in `MARKETS`) with a "two or more"
  row (`tdPlus`), for a back, a receiver, a tight end and, since app v102, a quarterback: a
  touchdown the player scores himself, running or catching, never a pass he throws, as DraftKings
  settles it and as `rowStats` and `espnStats` count it. Its chance is a logit capped by expected
  touches at `tdrate` x `tdmult`. A quarterback's is worded "Scores a rushing or receiving TD (not
  a TD pass)" (`tdLabel()`), and market + form gives it no second price (his rating is a passer's).
- **game bets** -- `ml` (to win), `ats` (to cover) and `total` (the game's points over or
  under `g.tot`, `pid:'game'`), one team bet and one total per game in the builder, a parlay of
  the panel's taking one team bet a game and no total, `grp:'TEAM'`. A total's chance is
  `totalBet()`: the market's own, mu the posted total and the over's chance its over and under
  prices with the margin out (`totalOver()`), 50% without both; `TOTAL_SD` (13.2, from
  2010-2025) prices only another number than the posted one, Normal about it. The model's own
  points (`modelTotal()`) are shown on the Game bets card as "model's points", never used: the
  study in `props/research/totals/README.md` found no points model that beat the posted total on
  2024-2026, and the rule used before (the model's points halfway to the total) worse than it.
  Its price is `g.tov`/`g.tou`, else -110 marked est.; it settles over, under or push in
  `settleGameLeg()`.
- **corr vs indep** -- `parlayProb()` returns both: `indep` is the legs
  multiplied naively, `corr` is a gaussian-copula Monte Carlo over the shipped
  pair correlation table. `corr` is the number the UI shows. It is seeded, so it
  is deterministic. A game total's pairs are `legRho()`'s own: with a player leg on
  the same game `TOTAL_RHO` by position and stat (measured on 2019-2024 finals), with a
  win or cover bet 0.
- **SGP pricing** -- `parlayDec()`. Legs sharing a game are priced as one
  same-game parlay (never longer than multiplying), legs across games multiply.
- **margin** -- the book's cut, `light`/`typical`/`heavy`, a visitor setting.
- **suggested parlays** -- two engines. `getPbTiers()` is the Parlay Builder's
  panel: Safe/Medium/Aggressive/Extreme, 2/3/4/5 legs (`PB_TIERS`, floors 25/12/5%
  after Safe), each found on its own from `pricedLegs(games)` (team bets and
  players, never a game total) on the ticked games and bet types (the Elo picks
  under it use `pricedLegs()` too), cached in
  `PB_CACHE`, outside `S`. `gameTiers()` is a game page's High,
  Medium and Low (`GAME_TIERS`: 2, 3 and 4 legs, paying at least +100/+250/+500 at
  35%/20%/10% or better, no leg shorter than `GAME_LEG_MIN`, -250), cached per game
  in `GAME_TIER_CACHE`, outside `S`. The panel's default levels are recorded per
  kickoff wave in `props/data/suggest_ledger.json` (the suggestions record).
- **liveWeek / weekOpen** -- the first week with an unfinished game; a later week
  is not "open" and shows fixtures only, because projections need recent form.
- **locked** -- kickoff has passed; the game stops accepting picks.

### The audit is a gate, not a test suite

`weekly.py` exits with an error when `audit.js` is not clean (its summary line read as
numbers by `audit_verdict`, 0 failures and 0 runtime errors, a missing line not clean;
`build/test_audit_gate.py` holds that to its cases), so the job stops before its commit step: a failing check means the site silently stops updating and the
scheduled run is marked failed. It does the same when a required download (schedule,
stats, roster, injury report) fails, or the stats would shrink -- both before any credit is
spent -- except that a 404 on the stats or the injury report, with none of this season's
published yet, is a file nflverse has not posted: the run publishes without it and says so.
Write checks against the app's invariants, never against whatever that week's
data happens to offer. Section V is the reality check: it builds the page the way a
browser does and compares it with the raw files the job downloaded (`PROPS_AUDIT_RAW=1`,
set by `weekly.py`) -- nobody on a reserve list listed, every Out off the page and every
Questionable tagged, every main line and price on the player the book named, the stats
complete, the teams the roster's, every price of this season's games -- and proves the rules
on made-up reports, so a bye, week 1, a week with no report yet or a pre-season with no
stats, no report and no prices (V0b, and the track-record checks that need prices only when
the payload has some) cannot fail it.

## Betting: how it fits together

The app is developed in a separate repo (`nfl-model-lab`) and shipped here as
one HTML file. `update.js` runs that real app headlessly in jsdom, through the
same upload path the owner would use by hand, starting from the last published
`state.json`, and writes the new state with the private parts stripped (bets,
bankroll, the owner's picks). It is idempotent: already-graded games are skipped,
games whose stats are not out yet wait for the next run.

`state.json` holds `teams`/`teamsH` (the team ratings and EPA of Alpha Model and of the
Challenger, `MODEL_H`: Power Ratings shows Alpha Model's EPA, but its order and Elo are the
team ratings of results in `elo/data/model.json`), `schedule`, `processed` (graded games), `odds` (nflverse's
moneylines, spread and total: the Pick'ems board's Vegas baseline), `qb`, `injuries`
(the report's columns the app reads), `depth`, `roster`, `events`, `joker`, `broly`,
`atKickoff` (each coming game's Alpha and Challenger call, recorded by every run before its
kickoff and kept from then on; the game is graded on it, `processed[gid].atKickoff`),
`jokerFit` (the weeks the Joker was fitted on after they were played, kept as data, and `calls`,
the history file their calls come from), `modelStatus` (only while
the Joker or Broly could not run: since when and why), `prevElo`/`prevRanks`, `published`, and
the app's own bookkeeping (`gamesPlayed`, `modelBuild`, `prevRanksWeek`, `season`,
`showAllModels`, `snapWeek`).

The app runs with the patches in `tools/patches.js`, in the job and on the page alike. Who starts
at quarterback: a quarterback who did not start his team's last game and was on that week's injury
report is held out until this week's report clears him (a Full or Limited practice, or a report his
team filed without him); Questionable with no practice on the final report is out. The depth chart
keeps an injured starter at the top and the game designations come only with the final report, so
without this every starter who missed the last game was counted back in from Tuesday (Mayfield,
Caleb Williams). The absences card names the starter a change replaced, says "not cleared" for a
held quarterback, and ends with the date of the injury report and depth chart it was built from,
flagging either when stale. A game abroad that nflverse codes 'Home' is neutral
(`neutral_sites.json`). The season's phase: once every posted game is played the card waits for
the next round, and after the Super Bowl it is empty. The smoke test holds in every phase: the
week before the opener (no game graded, no injury report yet), a week part played, between
playoff rounds, after the Super Bowl, and a season the Joker cannot call (`modelStatus`); a check
with nothing to look at says it was skipped. Team Rankings stars a team whose regular-season
finals outnumber its rating's record, or whose playoff final kicked off after the Elo file was
built (the Elo file's record is the regular season's); the note under the table says the whole
table predates that final, since every final re-scores every team's games.

Every call is frozen at kickoff: Alpha's and the Challenger's through `atKickoff`, the Joker's and
Broly's through `jobkit.freeze` (a game that has kicked off keeps the call of the last run before
it). A closing line nflverse posts afterwards, a depth chart that moves, or a refit never rewrites
a call a reader saw. The page shows the frozen call for a game under way and settles a scoreboard
final on it, for every model at once (the ELO Model and Broly included).

A required file that does not download fails `update.js` before anything is written: games.csv
always, the roster and depth chart from a week before the season, the injury report from its first
kickoff (the league files none before the week of the opener and nflverse's file is a 404 until it
does, so before then the run publishes without one and the absences card says "no injury report
yet"), both stats files once a final is a day and a half old (`patches.filesDue`, the one rule the
job and the smoke test both use). An older copy in `data/` is never used in its place. The
Joker and Broly refuse the same way on their own inputs (the season's stats and play-by-play once
a final is a day and a half old, Broly's prior), keep their last good picks, write `modelStatus`
and exit 1; the workflow carries on past them (`continue-on-error`) and the Pick'em Record says so.

**The Joker** (`betting/joker/`) is a separate gradient-boosted-trees model,
fitted 2019-2025 and refitted once with 2026 weeks 1-2 in its training (`tune_2026.py`, 24
September 2026), whose picks `joker.py` writes into `state.json` (`joker`, the pick
and home win chance for every game, regular season and playoffs, and `processed[gid].joker` once
graded) and show beside Alpha Model's on the Pick'em Record. Its calls on weeks 1-2 are not the
refit's, which saw those games' results (25-7, +2 against Vegas, in sample): on 12 October 2026 the
owner asked for the record it actually had, so `joker.py` takes those 32 games' calls on every run
from `betting/joker/history/2026_w1_w2.json`, written once from git and never by the job. Week 2's
are the calls `state.json` carried before each kickoff (`src: 'published'`, the commit and its time
on each); week 1 was played before the Joker existed (its model was fitted on 15 September, after the
week's last kickoff), so its calls are that original model's, fitted on 2019-2025 alone, called after
the games on the ratings from the games before each and the game's own closing lines (`src: 'backtest'`, the
same calls the state carried from its first commit). Its quarterback inputs give it no hindsight there:
the backup-quarterback drop is zero in a team's first game of a season, and the starters' names, which
games.csv records from the game itself, move none of the 16 chances when blanked (rescored from f0397e4's
model on 12 October 2026, every chance the file's to four places). Both are the model #117 put back. Week 1 is counted like any other week, as
the ELO Model's own backtest weeks 1-2 are on the same chart, so the models are compared on the same
games; `src` rides on each call and grade, and the record draws no hatch, label or note (the owner's
call of October 2026, kept). The real record: 13-3 in week 1, 11-5 in week 2, 24-8 and +1 against
Vegas (the refit showed 25-7, +2), one win fewer on the season than the refit showed. From week 3 the refit
model's calls were made before kickoff and stand. `joker.py` refuses a run where a week in
`fitted_weeks` has a game with no call in a history file, and the smoke test (5n) holds every call
of the file to the published state and its grade, and every call the last commit's copy of the file
held to that copy; 5h let such a call move onto the file's once, from the refit's call (which carries
no `src`), and holds it from then like any other call after its kickoff.
Each run also logs, in the job's
output, any input that has drifted from the scale it was fitted on (`drift.py`). It refuses a season more than
one past its frozen data.

The season is the app's (`freshState`), read by `patches.season()`: `update.js` downloads that
season's files and publishes `season`, which `joker.py` and `broly.py` read, so an early 2027
schedule in games.csv moves nothing until a 2027 app ships. The visitor's store is keyed on it.

Gate: the app's embedded model numbers must equal `reference_models.json` or the
publish aborts. Shipping a model change means passing the release gate in
`nfl-model-lab`, then copying both the app and `reference_models.json` here.

Tabs: AI Picks, Parlay Builder, Records, Bet Log, Power Ratings, Data Upload,
Backup (the build drops My Picks). The Bets and Stats page frames Records, Power
Ratings and Bet Log only, with the app's header and tab bar hidden; the Bet Log there is the X
Bet Log, read only (the page hands the frames `window.XBETS`; see "X's parlays and the X Bet Log").

## College: the test site

`cfb/` (X College Football Bets) is the NFL betting model's idea applied to college
football, as a separate site so it can be tried without touching the others:
moneylines and spreads only, no player props. Everything is ESPN's public feeds, free.

```
cfb/
  index.html          THE PAGE. hand-written, one file; fetches state.json on load
  state.json          generated by tools/update.js: the season, rated, called and graded
  data/history.json   2014 to last season, every game with an FBS team in it (tools/history.js, once;
                      the job appends the season just finished on its first run of a new one)
  data/teams.json     ESPN's team table: names, colours, logos, conference (the job refreshes it)
  data/model.json     the fitted parameters (tools/fit.js)
  tools/espn.js       the feeds: scoreboard by week, rankings, conferences; a game's status
                      (pre, live, final, postponed), a TBD kickoff time, a TBD opponent
  tools/season.js     the season in play and the clock, from one place (CFB_SEASON, CFB_NOW for tests)
  tools/elo.js        THE MODEL: Elo with home field, a margin multiplier, preseason regression,
                      a points-per-Elo spread and a normal margin for cover chances
  tools/fit.js        grid search on the history, last season held out
  tools/update.js     the job; tools/news.js the CFB News tab's file; tools/smoke.js the gate
  tools/simulate.js   the job over fabricated weeks, the playoff and a rollover, offline (the gate's
                      other half); tools/fixtures.js writes ESPN's feeds in their shapes for it
  news.json           generated: the week's slate as a newsletter, a card and a window per game
```

The job replays the history and this season's finals in date order, so the ratings
are current; a game still to come gets the model's chance, spread and, from ESPN,
DraftKings' line as ESPN carries it at that run, all frozen from its kickoff (by ESPN's status
or the clock, whichever first; a TBD time from the start of its day) and kept after it, so the
record grades what the page showed. A line ESPN stops carrying is dropped from a game to come,
never kept as if current, with one exception: a line read within the 24 hours before the game's
kickoff is held (`line.held`, dated by its `at`) so a line ESPN leaves out on the last runs before
kickoff is still the one the game is graded on; the page shows it as not current, with
no call and no price, and after kickoff as the last line before it; CFB News cites it as the last
one read, with its time. Games over before the job first ran are graded from the replay and
marked; the page leads with the calls made before kickoff, beside DraftKings' favourite on the
same games, and shows FBS against FBS and the replayed calls apart. A postponed game offers nothing and is not graded; the line frozen before it is kept aside
(`heldLine`), so a game suspended and finished later is graded on it. A game that kicked off with a
frozen call and that ESPN then stops listing stays in the state (`gone`), its call and line with it,
and says so on its card. A TBD opponent is shown without a call and counts for no one. A side against
the spread is taken only when the model and the line differ by three points or more, and never
against a lower-division opponent. The current week is the first regular-season week with a game
to play, then the postseason; `phase` says opening, regular, postseason or over.

A failed feed never publishes as the season: a week's scoreboard that does not download is
carried from the last publish (said so in `notes`, shown under the header) only when every game
in it is final or more than five days off; otherwise the run exits 1 before the commit. A
scoreboard with under half the games the last publish had for its week stops the run too; a
graded game ESPN stops listing is kept. Polls that fail are carried and dated, and so is a single
poll the feed stops carrying in the same season (the committee's ranking, which seeds the byes);
a poll from another season is not this one's. The first run of a new season appends the finished
one to `data/history.json` and refuses the new season when that pull throws, has a week with no
finals (week 1, a week between two weeks with games, or the postseason) or has fewer finals in a
week than the last publish of that season had (`history.js` holesOf and shortOf). State files
carry `schema: 2` and `run` (the minute the job read ESPN, the `at` of every current line).

The playoff picture is three thousand simulations of the rest of the season: each
conference's title game, a committee proxy (rating less a cost per loss, the polls
where they exist), the five best-placed champions plus seven at large, straight seeding,
the bracket played out. Once the field is decided (`bracket.final`: the conference title games
over, with nothing left to play before the last of them, so Army-Navy a week later does not hold
it open) the odds play the bracket shown, its twelve in and its top four on a bye; once
ESPN lists the playoff's first round and byes, they play the real bracket (seeded by the
committee's ranking), a played playoff game keeps its result and a team out has no title chance.
Tabs: Games, Rankings, Power Ratings, Playoff, Parlays (browser
only, no sync; a game that has kicked off by the visitor's clock, or whose line is older than the
job's last run, is never offered, and live scores are read from ESPN in the browser), Record, and
CFB News: the week's slate as the NFL tracker shows it, a tile per
game (kickoff, TV, venue, a note) opening a window with each side's matchup bullets, last five
results and a stat breakdown in divided bars. Nobody writes forty college previews a week, so
`tools/news.js` writes the note and the bullets from the numbers: ESPN's game summary (scoring
and yardage per game and allowed, last five results, FPI, the spread record, leaders,
injuries), its team statistics (yards per play, third downs, sacks, turnovers, penalties), the
model's call and the frozen line; `news.json` carries what the page draws plus what the gate
holds the bullets to (each side's leaders, injury list and headlines). A team's headlines are
football's only, from after its last game and naming no opponent it has already played; a season
leader out on the injury report or in a headline is marked out; sacks are compared a game. A
feed that does not answer leaves its part out and the window says which; a game summary that does
not answer keeps the last run's preview, dated, its line read from this run's state (a line held for
the grade is cited as the last read). A game's preview is kept once it has started (by
ESPN or the clock).

`.github/workflows/cfb.yml` runs 17 times a week (daily 06:17 and 13:47 UTC, Saturday 10:17 and

## Hockey: the NBA Hub's idea, on this repo's shape

`nhl/` (X NHL Bets) is the college site's shape (one page, one job, one state file) with the
NBA Hub's unit and look: the NHL has no weeks, so the page opens on a day strip, yesterday to
ten days out, one card per game. Moneylines, puck lines and totals; no player props. Everything
is ESPN's public feeds, free.

```
nhl/
  index.html          THE PAGE. hand-written, one file; fetches state.json on load
  state.json          generated by tools/update.js: the season, rated, called and graded
  data/history.json   2011-12 to 2025-26, every game with its period count (tools/history.js, once)
  data/season_<y>.json  each season the job closed itself, written by the first run of the next one
                      from the last state of it (tools/hist.js reads both, for every script)
  data/teams.json     the fixed club table (code, ESPN id, conference, division) plus names, colours, logos from the feed
  data/model.json     the fitted parameters (tools/fit.js)
  tools/espn.js       the feed: one scoreboard a day; the club table; DraftKings' three markets
  tools/elo.js        THE MODEL: Elo with home ice, back-to-back, rest, an overtime weight and a
                      goal-margin multiplier; a Poisson goals layer for the puck line and the total
  tools/fit.js        coordinate search on the history, last season held out
  tools/update.js     the job (--scores, its first pass: scores and schedule, every call copied, none made)
  tools/smoke.js      the gate: the page and the state against the injury report, the box scores,
                      DailyFaceoff, the clock and the last published state
  tools/simulate.js   the whole job offline on fixtures in the feeds' shapes, run first by the workflow
  tools/sources.js    the job's last step: fails the run, after the commit, when a source broke
  tools/fetch_box.js  ESPN box scores into data/box_<season>.jsonl (2021-22 on, each line with its
                      score) and data/injuries.json (the id read from the player's link)
  tools/starters.js   DailyFaceoff's announced goalies into data/starters.json, each game with its
                      date and ESPN id; a game matching nothing on the schedule kept aside, never
                      applied (GitHub's runners only)
  tools/players.js    THE PLAYER MODEL: skaters' offence and defence, goalies' saves, lineups by ice
                      time; fit, holdout, walk-forward, every game to come lined up -> data/players.json
```

The job reads every day of the season (skipping the days already complete in the last state; a
day through tomorrow with a game to finish or call must answer, or the run exits 1 having written
nothing), replays the finished seasons and this season's finals in date order, and gives every
game still to come the model's chance, expected margin and total, the overtime chance, and
DraftKings' moneyline, puck line and total. That call is frozen at puck drop by the clock (a game
ESPN still has as scheduled past its start, or has moved less than half a day later after it, is not
called again, and the smoke test holds it as under way, not to this run's lineups) and every part of
it is copied after, so the record grades what the page showed; rows an older job recomputed after puck drop are
put back once to the call shown. The job runs `update.js` twice: the first pass (`--scores`) reads the
same days, writes the scores and the schedule and copies every call as it was, so `fetch_box.js` boxes
last night's finals and `players.js` lines up on the schedule the second pass calls on; only the second
makes calls. A game comes off the schedule only on the feed's word (postponed, cancelled, or moved to
another day); one an answer leaves out is kept with its call, line and grade (`kept`), a blank answer
on a day the last state has games on is no answer (unless the answer has each of them on another day,
the same id: they moved), and what came off is in `removed` with the reason. A side is taken only where the model's chance beats the book's
implied by five points; the record counts straight-up calls on every game and the sides taken on
each market. The playoff picture, in the regular season, is two thousand simulations of the rest
of it (overtime and its loser point from the goals layer), the league's bracket (top three a
division, two wild cards a conference), four best-of-seven rounds with home ice to the higher seed;
once the regular season is over the field is the real one and each series ESPN has a game in is
played on from its real score, so a club knocked out has no chance left and the bracket shows every
series' score (`phase`: preseason, regular, postseason, over, offseason). The field is then the
sixteen clubs of ESPN's first round, its seeds and wild cards read off the bracket: the table breaks
ties only to goal difference, so a club it left out that ESPN has in a series is in for certain, and
the club it displaced is out.

WHO PLAYS (`players.js`): each game's lineup is who dressed last less anyone ESPN's injury report has
out, on injured reserve or suspended, or lists under another club; the goalie, game by game, is
DailyFaceoff's for that game and date, else the club's most frequent starter in its last ten games
this season, the other goalie on the second night of a back to back (other than the box score's
starter of the night before, or the one expected for it while it is not boxed). The page's clock decides what
has started: the Games tab's Today is the visitor's Eastern date, a game past its puck drop offers no
bet and says it is under way, a live score the job published says when it was read (and the page
reads ESPN itself once on opening for a game under way), and a leg whose game has started leaves the
builder. Tabs: Games, Standings, Power Ratings, Playoff, Players (the player model's rankings and
the sources it stands on), Parlays (browser only, no sync), Record.

`.github/workflows/nhl.yml` runs five times a day, each slot queued for the window it must land in
at the 2.7 to 8.7 hours late GitHub starts this repo's runs, and once on a push to `nhl/tools`, the page
or the workflow: `simulate.js`, `update.js --scores`,
`fetch_box.js`, `starters.js`, `players.js`, `update.js`, `smoke.js`, the commit (`nhl/state.json`,
`nhl/data/teams.json`, `nhl/data/box_*.jsonl`, `nhl/data/injuries.json`, `nhl/data/starters.json`,
`nhl/data/players.json` and any `nhl/data/season_*.json`), then `sources.js`, which fails the run
after the commit when a source the state stands on broke.

## News: the half that is not automated

`pull-week.js` refreshes scores, records and stat bars from ESPN and
TeamRankings, and drafts `data/weekN.js`. **The narrative is written by a
person.** A draft is not live until it is added to `data/weeks.js` and
`index.html`. Read `news/HANDOFF.md` before touching anything here. The narrative
never quotes the Deep Dive's or the rank chip's ranks (they move under it; the
smoke test fails a live week that does). After week 18 the tracker stays on
week 18 and says the regular season is complete; it does not cover the playoffs.

## Gotchas

- Editing a generated file is wasted work: the next scheduled run overwrites it.
  Change the source and rebuild.
- `betting/app/x_nfl_betting_model.html` looks generated but is **source**.
- The props audit needs `npm ci` in `props/build/` first.
- `parlayProb()` is seeded and deterministic; don't assume Monte Carlo noise
  explains a changed number.
