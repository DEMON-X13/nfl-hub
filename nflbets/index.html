What each file does

  • Player stats is the one that matters. Every player's form, every team's volume and every defense update from it. It only exists once games have been played, so before Week 1 that link returns "not found", which is expected.
  • Rosters keep everyone on the right team. The 2026 offseason is already built in, so Week 1 is correct out of the box, but signings and cuts happen all season and this is what catches them.
  • Injury report pulls anyone ruled out or doubtful off this week's game pages so you aren't looking at a player who isn't dressing.
  • Depth charts set who counts as the starter, so a game page shows QB1 and RB1 rather than whoever happened to touch the ball most last season. It's the big file, and it changes slowly, so every few weeks is enough.
  • Prices are not on this list because nothing here fetches them. They come from the odds API on the Thursday and Saturday builds, and the panel above says when credits were last spent on them.
  • Re-uploading is safe. Weeks already counted are skipped.

What's changed

A running log of fixes and features, most recent first. If something looks off, check here before assuming it's new. Bold entries are bugs that were producing wrong numbers rather than just looking wrong.

v28A game now opens in a window over the games list instead of replacing it. Close it with the button, the Escape key, or a click outside; the list underneath keeps its place and any legs you ticked show there straight away.
v27Added the Track Record tab. Every threshold, book line and touchdown chance shown before kickoff is scored against the result and grouped by confidence band, by the percentage the model said, by week, by market, and for your locked parlays. The chances are frozen at grade time alongside the projections, so a played game is scored on exactly what it showed. Older graded weeks are scored from their frozen projection with the same tables. Each table carries a noise margin so a thin sample is not read as a trend.
v19The header of an expanded player had a rounded corner on one side and a square one on the other, with its border cut across the join. Caused by a typo, border-bottom-radius, which is not a real CSS property so browsers ignored it silently. Swept the rest of the stylesheet for the same mistake.
v18The week selector opened on week 2 because it picked the first week you hadn't uploaded stats for, and you had uploaded week 1's. It now reads the schedule: the current week is the earliest one still holding an unplayed game. Weeks after that stay closed, since a projection built on form from games that haven't happened would look authoritative and be stale. Finished-game stats moved from a table to tiles, with colour on the numbers rather than the row. Saved parlays gained a proper money panel: staked, price, payout, profit, chance, result, plus a running total across every ticket.
v17A played game read STARTED forever because nothing marked it finished. Games are now final once a score is in, or four hours after kickoff. Pulled the real result in, so week 1 shows NE 10 at SEA 13. Finished games drop the betting ladders entirely and show projected against actual instead.
v16Games lock at kickoff: no checkboxes, no adding legs, unsaved legs from a started game drop out of the builder. Added save and lock for parlays, which freezes legs, stake and price and moves the ticket to a Saved section that grades itself when stats arrive. Fixed a real error found while testing: a finished game's projection was being recomputed after that week's results were folded in, so it drifted toward the answer. Projections are now frozen before grading, so a recap shows what the model actually said beforehand.
v15The price sheet download built a link that was never attached to the page, which Chrome tolerates and Safari silently drops. Fixed, along with the same pattern on the backup export. Added a sheet covering just your parlay legs, since the full-week version is nearly five thousand rows.
v14Ran a full audit across every game, player and threshold in the season, 26,800 checks. It found three real bugs. The anytime-touchdown cap had silently switched off during a data rebuild, which had been holding backup running backs down from absurd numbers. Market anchoring failed on 40 of 303 lines, because the search assumed model and market were within twice each other, and Jacksonville's lead back differs by 2.6 times. The outcome distribution jumped at its internal tier boundaries, which would also have made odds twitch as a projection drifted. All fixed; the build now refuses to produce a file with any required piece missing.
v13Added estimated sportsbook prices to every threshold. Where a real line exists the distribution is shifted to agree with the market at that number, vig stripped first; elsewhere the average measured gap is applied. Then a bookmaker's cut goes on top, adjustable light, typical or heavy. Baked in 303 real Week 1 lines with both prices, and made the main line itself bettable on both sides.
v12Every threshold now shows what its chance is worth as a price, labelled fair since it carries no bookmaker margin.
v11The home page was unreadable on a phone. The game row is a seven-column grid and the mobile rule only told two of those seven where to go, so the rest landed wherever; the projections column was also being hidden by a leftover rule. Every cell is now placed explicitly as a stacked card.
v10Wired in the team model from the other project. Tested our own points model first and it lost to the betting market, 7.60 average error against 7.25, so the market is used wherever a line exists and our models only fill the 172 games without one. Those weeks previously assumed a flat league-average game.
v9Added the Parlay Builder with checkboxes on every projection. One pick per player per stat, enforced by how legs are stored, so choosing 30+ after 20+ replaces it. Corrected a misleading display: the first version showed an expected return even with no real odds loaded, computed from the model's own margin-free prices, which made every parlay look profitable. It now refuses to compute one until a real price is entered.
v8Filled the empty space on the games list with the three biggest projections per game, plus total points and the winner.
v7Team tags took the colours from the other project, including the rule that falls through to a team's secondary colour when the primary is too dark to read as anything but navy.
v6Players from the wrong teams. The saved copy in your browser was overriding the newly baked-in rosters, because the version check only looked at whether the model had changed, not the roster data. Roster data now carries its own stamp, and uploading a roster removes players no longer on one instead of only adding and moving.
v5Baked in the 2026 rosters and depth charts: 97 players changed teams, 97 more were released or retired and dropped. Depth charts set who counts as a starter, so pages show QB1 and RB1 rather than whoever touched the ball most last season, and name the starters who can't be projected because they have no NFL history.
v4Rebuilt around collapsible players and threshold ladders. Removed the betting tabs. Fixed backups appearing as starters, including Mac Jones alongside Purdy: players are now ranked by projected usage within their position and only the starter and main rotation show.
v3Reworked what the app flags. The earlier version measured edge against the de-vigged line, which tells you where you disagree with a sportsbook but not whether you'd profit. It now measures against the price you actually pay, cut included. Also fixed the odds template writing market names its own parser rejected, so those rows were silently dropped.
v2Added correlated parlay pricing after measuring how every pair of markets moves together across 2019 to 2024. Multiplying legs is off by 14 points on the strongest pairs.
v1Built the projection model from 124,000 player-games, 2019 to 2025. The first version was badly wrong about probability: it assumed a bell curve around each projection and said 50% where the real rate was 44%, because player stats are right-skewed. Replaced with the measured shape of each stat's outcomes. A second bug had backup running backs at 76% to score, from an unshrunk career rate: one touchdown in two games looked elite. Fixed with shrinkage toward the league average plus a cap tied to expected touches.

Backup

  • Save backup now writes a file with your saved parlays, the parlay builder and your stake. Those live only in this browser: clearing site data or switching computers loses them, and nothing backs them up automatically.
  • Import backup restores one of those files, or moves your parlays to another computer.
  • Stats, rosters, injuries and prices are not your data to lose: the GitHub job bakes them into the page and every load picks them up.

The note above turns red once your last backup is more than a week old. Backups land in your Downloads folder.
